Welcome to the Infinite Archive

The Infinite Archive

Somewhere past the last shelf of the last library there is an archive with no end. Every power anyone ever imagined is written on a scroll inside it, and you have been handed a key.

Draw a scroll and it gives up its secret. An ability is learned the moment you read it. A trait is woven into you by the scroll's own magic. Words of summoning lift off the page and call a companion, a weapon, a vehicle, or a whole world into being. The archive decides what you find. What you do with it is yours.

Keys

You open the archive with keys. A key's star level decides which shelves it reaches: each level draws from three neighbouring ranks of scroll. Keys are earned by doing things in your story; the harder the deed, the brighter the key.

1 Star (F–D)
A hard but human deed: win a fight, land a job, hunt a wolf
2 Star (E–C)
Peak human or faintly supernatural: a fortune in a day, a gang brought down
3 Star (D–B)
Seriously difficult: rob a guarded vault, win over someone famous, kill a demon
4 Star (C–A)
Nearly impossible: escape certain death, slay a dragon, pacify a great enemy
5 Star (B–S)
Extraordinary: save a city, halt an army, charm a devil's spouse
6 Star (A–SS)
Legendary: kill a legendary dragon, tear the wings off an angel, save a nation or a planet
7 Star (S–SSS)
Mythical: break a prophecy, seduce death, shake the world
EX Star (SS–EX)
Impossible: save the universe, beat every hero, make a star, unmake a galaxy

Starting: how your character finds the archive
Finding the archive

The archive is a framework for a story, not a rulebook. Run it however your story needs. The one thing every character has in common is a first handful of keys, and how many you get depends on how you got in.

Ways in

Thrown in by a god, an eldritch thing, or a wizard with a grudge

Someone with power and poor manners dropped you into a world with the key already in your hand. One 4-star key and two 3-star keys, with their apologies.

A new world, no wizard involved

A truck, a stroke of luck, a door that should not have opened. Two 3-star keys and three 1-star keys.

Native to the world you chose

You, as an ordinary citizen of that setting. Nobody important, until now. Three 3-star keys.

You woke up as an existing character

The story's lead gets one 1-star key. A secondary character gets two 2-star keys. A bit-part character gets three 3-star keys. The further from the spotlight, the more the archive owes you.

Someone in that world finds the key

No insert at all: a character who already lives there wakes with the archive open to them. Two 2-star keys for someone the plot cares about, three 3-star keys for someone it doesn't.

Want it all drawn for you? Open the wizard.

On drawing honestly

Nobody is watching. If a draw would wreck the story, draw again, or pick the scroll you needed and call it luck. The archive is here to make writing more fun, not to referee it.

Curses

Beside the archive sits a tome of curses. Read one and bind it to your character, at the start of the story or whenever things need shaking up, and the tome pays you in keys. The worse the curse, the better the keys. Bind too many and you will be absurdly powerful before chapter two, which is less fun than it sounds.

Using this in your work

Write whatever you like with it. A mention of the Infinite Archive somewhere is appreciated and nothing more is asked. The site's code is free software under the GPL, so you can host your own copy too.

Abilities, skills, and how far they go

An ability is anything you have to actively use. If you like structure, give your character ability slots: start with one and add another for every five abilities drawn. If you don't, ignore that entirely.
