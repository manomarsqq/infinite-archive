The Infinite Archive

Somewhere past the last shelf of the last library there is an archive with no end. Every power anyone ever imagined is written on a scroll inside it, and you have been handed a key.

Draw a scroll and it gives up its secret. An ability is learned the moment you read it. A trait is woven into you by the scroll's own magic. Words of summoning lift off the page and call a companion, a weapon, a vehicle, or a whole world into being. The archive decides what you find. What you do with it is yours.

Ways in

The archive is a framework for a story, not a rulebook. Run it however the story needs. The one thing every character shares is a first handful of keys, and how many depends on how the key reached them.

Inherited

The key turned up among a dead relative's effects, with no note and no explanation. You have the keys they never used: two 3-star keys and one 2-star key.

Chosen at random

The archive picks someone every so often, from everyone alive, for no reason anyone has found. You got the letter. One 4-star key, one 2-star key, one 1-star key.

Stolen

It belonged to a collector, a wizard, a vault. Now it belongs to you, and someone may want it back. Keys are small and plentiful on the way out: three 2-star keys and two 1-star keys.

Sent

A faction that knows what the shelves hold put the key in your hand and told you what to bring back. They equipped you well: one 4-star key and one 3-star key, and they will be expecting results.

Died

The archive is what came after. You woke on its floor with a key in your fist and a second life to spend. Three 3-star keys.

Keys

You open the archive with keys, and the metal of the key tells you how deep into the stacks it reaches: each level draws from three neighbouring ranks of scroll. Keys are not given, they are earned by deeds in your story. Decide what your character did, decide how big it was, and the archive hands over the matching key. Putting the darkest key next to the humblest one is deliberate.

Every draw lands in the codex's ledger with the key it was drawn with and the time. If you drew without a key, or the key came from somewhere else, set it there afterwards and write down what earned it. The ledger is on the page, and each Draw page has its own.

Iron 1★ · F–D

Black, matte, a little worn. A hard day's work: anything a determined person can finish with effort and no help.

Silver 2★ · E–C

Beyond most people: a feat the town talks about for a week.

Blue steel 3★ · D–B

Dangerous: something that could plausibly kill you, done anyway.

Gold 4★ · C–A

Heroic: surviving what should not be survivable, or ending what should not be endable.

Platinum 5★ · B–S

White and bright, the last key that still looks like something a jeweller could make. Something a city remembers for a generation.

Crystal 6★ · A–SS

Clear as glass, with light moving slowly inside it like a flame seen through water. Something a nation writes into its history.

Star-iron 7★ · S–SSS

Black like the iron key, but full of tiny points of light, a piece of the night sky cut into a key. Something the whole world feels happen.

No key at all EX★ · SS–EX

A key-shaped hole in the air, with light coming through from whatever is on the other side. You turn the gap. Something reality itself has to adjust to.

Abilities and skills

An ability is anything you have to actively use. If you like structure, give your character ability slots: start with one and add another for every five abilities drawn. If you don't, ignore that entirely.

Some skill scrolls name a level. This is what the levels mean:

Beginner
A passing knowledge and perhaps a year of on-and-off practice
Apprentice
About five years of solid basic training
Journeyman
About ten years of dedicated training
Expert
Roughly twenty-five years of practical experience, all at once
Master
A century of skill, written straight into you
Grandmaster
A master of masters; nobody alive can match you
Divine
You invented the field, and everyone else is imitating you badly

Curses

Beside the archive sits a tome of curses. Read one and bind it to your character, at the start of the story or whenever things need shaking up, and the tome pays you in keys. The worse the curse, the better the keys. Bind too many and you will be absurdly powerful before chapter two, which is less fun than it sounds.

Drawing honestly

Nobody is watching. If a draw would wreck the story, draw again, or pick the scroll you needed and call it luck. The archive is here to make writing more fun, not to referee it.
